A Little of Everything
Dart, no widgets → A list that reacts → A layout that survives → A screen that answers → A form that validates
Week 8 has no new lecture — it is the midterm week — so this lab is a recap of weeks 1–7 in one small app, a reading list. One level per topic, in the order the midterm asks them: a Dart task with no widgets, a widget with state, a layout that must not overflow, a navigation that brings a value back, and a form that validates itself. Nothing here is new. Everything here is the kind of thing you will be asked to do with the Wi-Fi off.
The app: a Book has a title, an author, a page count, an optional year and a read flag. The list shows the books with a filter, a tap opens the detail, the detail can mark a book as read or delete it, and a button adds a new one through a form. Six files by the end: main.dart, models/book.dart, widgets/book_tile.dart, library_screen.dart, detail_screen.dart, add_book_screen.dart.

Tasks (5)
1.	Level 1: Dart, no widgets
Move Book and seed to lib/models/book.dart and finish the model. book.dart imports nothing from Flutter — it is plain Dart:
•A named constructor Book.fromMap(Map<String, dynamic> m): title, author and pages are required keys (m['pages'] as int); year is m['year'] as int? — a missing key stays null; read is m['read'] as bool? ?? false. No ! anywhere
•String toString() → Null Safety for Humans — B. Seitkali, 196 pp. (2022); when year is null the parentheses are not printed at all. One if, or one ?:, on a nullable — the thing week 2 was about
•Three top-level functions in the same file: int totalPages(List<Book> books) with fold; List<Book> unread(List<Book> books) with where and toList(); List<String> authors(List<Book> books) with map, toSet() and toList() — no duplicates, original order
•In main(), before runApp: debugPrint('${totalPages(seed)} pages, ${unread(seed).length} unread'); and debugPrint(Book.fromMap({'title': 'X', 'author': 'Y', 'pages': 10}).toString());
The test of whether you did this right:
•	The console prints 1220 pages, 4 unread and X — Y, 10 pp. — no parentheses, no null in the output.
•Now break it on purpose: change final int? year to final int year. The compiler points at the seed, at fromMap and at toString — every place that relied on the question mark. That is the promise int? makes and int does not. Put the ? back.
2.	Level 2: A list that reacts
lib/library_screen.dart: a StatefulWidget whose state is the list and the filter:
•final List<Book> _books = [...seed]; — a copy, so the seed is never touched. enum Filter { all, unread, read } above the class; Filter _filter = Filter.all; in State
•A getter List<Book> get _visible with a switch expression over _filter: all → _books, unread → unread(_books) from Level 1, read → _books.where((b) => b.read).toList()
•A Row of three ChoiceChips — All, Unread, Read — above the list; selected: _filter == f, onSelected → setState. Below it an Expanded ListView.builder over _visible with a CheckboxListTile per book: title, author as subtitle, value: book.read, onChanged: (v) => setState(() => book.read = v ?? false)
•The AppBar title shows the count: Reading list (4) — from _visible.length, so it changes with the filter
The test of whether you did this right:
•Tick a book under Unread: it disappears from the list and the title drops by one. All shows five. Switch filters back and forth: the chip stays selected and the ticks are remembered.
•Now break it on purpose: write book.read = v ?? false without setState. Tap the checkbox: nothing moves. The value changed; nobody rebuilt. Put setState back.
3.	Level 3: A layout that survives
Replace CheckboxListTile with your own BookTile — a StatelessWidget in lib/widgets/book_tile.dart, const constructor, final Book book, final VoidCallback onToggle — and add a header above the chips:
•BookTile is a Card with 12 px Padding and a Row: a Stack on the left — a CircleAvatar with the first letter of the title and, when the book is unread, a small Positioned dot in the top-right corner — then SizedBox(width: 12), then an Expanded Column (crossAxisAlignment.start): the title in textTheme.titleMedium, maxLines: 2, overflow: TextOverflow.ellipsis, and under it author · 196 pp.; then a Checkbox on the right that calls onToggle
•The header: a Card with a Row of three Expanded columns — Books, Pages, Unread — the number in textTheme.headlineSmall, the word in bodySmall. The numbers come from Level 1: _books.length, totalPages(_books), unread(_books).length
•Every colour comes from Theme.of(context).colorScheme and every text style from textTheme. No Colors.* and no fontSize: anywhere in book_tile.dart
The test of whether you did this right:
•The long title wraps to two lines and ends with …; no yellow-and-black stripes; the checkbox is at the same x on every row. Tick a book: the dot disappears and the header's Unread drops by one.
•Now break it on purpose: remove Expanded from around the Column. Stripes, "overflowed by N pixels". The Row gave the Column unbounded width and the Text took all of it. Put Expanded back. Then rotate the emulator: the three header columns still share the width equally — that is the other thing Expanded does.
4.	Level 4: A screen that answers
lib/detail_screen.dart: a StatelessWidget that receives its Book through the constructor and answers with what the user decided:
•Tapping a BookTile pushes DetailScreen(book: book) with MaterialPageRoute. The detail shows every field; year prints as Year unknown when it is null. No BackButton, no leading: — the arrow is drawn by AppBar
•enum BookAction { toggleRead, delete } in detail_screen.dart. Two buttons: "Mark as read" / "Mark as unread" (the label depends on book.read) pops with BookAction.toggleRead; "Delete" first asks with showDialog<bool> + AlertDialog "Delete 'title'?" — Cancel / Delete — and only on true pops with BookAction.delete. context.mounted between the dialog's await and the pop
•The list side: final action = await Navigator.of(context).push<BookAction>(...); then if (!mounted) return; then a switch on action: null → nothing; toggleRead → setState flips book.read; delete → setState removes the book. The detail never touches _books — it only answers
The test of whether you did this right:
•Open a book, press the back arrow: nothing changes. Open it, Delete, Cancel: still on the detail. Delete, Delete: back on the list, the row is gone, the header's Books is one less. Mark as read from the detail: the tick in the list is set when you come back. The console stays clean through all of it.
•Draw the stack before you run it: list → detail → dialog. The dialog pops with a bool, the detail pops with a BookAction, the list is still there underneath, unchanged, waiting.
5.	Level 5: A form that validates
lib/add_book_screen.dart: a FloatingActionButton on the list pushes it with push<Book>, and it answers with a Book:
•A Form with a GlobalKey<FormState> in State; four controllers — title, author, pages, year — fields of State, disposed. TextFormFields with a validator each: title at least 2 characters after trim(); author not empty; pages: int.tryParse is not null and the number is above 0 — final n = int.tryParse(v ?? ''); if (n == null || n <= 0) return 'A whole number above 0';; year: empty is fine, otherwise a number between 1450 and DateTime.now().year
•keyboardType: TextInputType.number on pages and year; textInputAction.next on the first three and .done on the last; onTapOutside folds the keyboard; the Column sits in a SingleChildScrollView; autovalidateMode.onUserInteraction on the Form
•Save: validate(); if true, build the Book from the controllers — int.parse(_pages.text) is safe now, the validator proved it; int.tryParse(_year.text) for the year — tryParse('') returns null, which is exactly what an empty year means — and pop(book). The list awaits, checks mounted, setState(() => _books.add(book))
•flutter analyze prints No issues found!; dart format . has been run; no ! anywhere except currentState! in the Save handler
The test of whether you did this right:
•Save on the empty form: three messages at once, under their fields, none for year. Pages abc: a message, no pop. Year empty: saves, and the detail says Year unknown. Back on the list the new book is there, unread, and the header's Books, Pages and Unread all grew.
•Back arrow on the form: nothing is added, nothing crashes — null came back, and if (book == null) return; is the whole handling.
